Credit where due

✓ Problem-to-solution framing

The four founder situations (short on hands / adoption slowing / need MVP / not investor-ready) is exactly the Eleken move. Strong.

Real client messages feel authentic in a way polished quote cards don't. Good instinct — just curate them (see slide 6).

Ship-blocker · 01

The problem-solution videos are random strangers.

Each of the four founder-case cards — Extend My Team, Redesign, Ship MVP, Polish for Investors — plays a video of an unrelated content creator: "Pitol In Motion," "Dani Lee," "Adriana Girdler," a woman in a bedroom.

To a SaaS founder, this reads instantly as stock filler — or worse, fake testimonials. It actively destroys the trust the copy works so hard to build.

This is the single most damaging thing on the site.

The fix
  • Replace with real product UI / case screenshots from the actual project
  • Or kill the video entirely — a clean static illustration beats a fake-looking clip
  • If you want motion, use a short looping screen-recording of your own work

Non-negotiable before launch.

Strategic gap

Where is the "AI-native" in your AI-native repositioning?

You told me the entire point of this revamp is transforming from a UI/UX agency into an AI-native product design studio. I read the whole site. That story is almost completely absent.

What the site says today
  • "Enterprise-grade UX frameworks with custom branding"
  • Why-choose-us = pragmatism, metrics, transparency
  • "Build-ready UX, not concept decks"
  • All good — but all generic agency virtues
What's missing
  • How AI changes the way you design
  • Why that makes you faster / cheaper / better
  • The contrast: old way vs AI-native way
  • Concrete proof — cycles, variations, iteration speed

Right now this is an excellent pragmatic agency site — not an AI-native one. A visitor would never know you've repositioned. This needs a dedicated section (see structure, slide 8–9).

Worth fixing before launch

The smaller stuff that adds up

!

Typos in client-facing copy

"Proccess" (nav), "Enterprice" (project card), "Bahance" (footer). A prospect's literal thought: "if they can't proofread their own site, what's their QA like?" Sweep every section.

~

Identical stats on every client card

Every featured client says "Launch in 1 month / Reduce cost 30%" — copy-paste placeholder. Vary with real per-project numbers, or remove.

~

Testimonials = politeness, not results

Several are mid-project pleasantries ("I'll review and get back to you"). Curate for the ones that talk about outcomes: funding raised, drop-off fixed, time-to-launch cut.

~

Stats section floats in a dead void

95+ / 100+ / 20+ / 57% sit in a huge empty dark block. Numbers feel lost. Tighten the spacing so they read as a confident strip.

i

Awards = mostly "Nominee"

Fine as a trust strip, but don't oversell "Design Excellence" off nominations. Honest framing protects you.

Technical

Test the motion on a real phone before you trust it.

Several sections (the rock-texture block after the hero, "Featured Cases", "Built for Industries") render almost entirely empty until you scroll into them, and the scroll-pinning makes the page feel like it's fighting the user.

On desktop with a fast connection it's fine. On a mid-range Android or a throttled network, this can look broken.

QA checklist
  • Test on a real Infinix / Vivo, not just the M1 Pro
  • Throttle to "Fast 3G" in DevTools and re-scroll
  • Check the scroll-pinning doesn't trap the user
  • Confirm reveal animations fire on first paint, not late

Awwwards-grade motion is only an asset if it's smooth. If it stutters, premium motion reads cheap — the opposite of the intent.

The recommended structure

The core principle for the new site

Cuberto = the 8 seconds of wow

Lives in the hero and portfolio only. Big type, motion, dark canvas, magnetic feel. This is what makes them stop scrolling.

Eleken = the 8 minutes of trust

Governs everything below the fold and the whole site architecture. Problem-agitation copy, industry pages, proof, SEO surface. This is what makes them book a call.

Your two references pull in opposite directions on purpose. Keep them in their lanes. If Cuberto's motion-first, copy-light philosophy bleeds into the whole homepage, you get a beautiful site that ranks for nothing and converts founders poorly.

Recommended structure

05Problem → Solution KEEPThe four founder cases. Fix the videos (slide 4).

07Services by stage KEEPSeed / Growth / Scale. Re-cut the language through the AI-native lens.
08Industries → detail pages KEEPYour SEO engine. Build as real, indexable pages — not anchor links. 4–6 verticals you can show work in.
09How we work TRIMCompress to a tight 4–5 step flow on homepage. Full detail on a /how-we-work page.
10Testimonials KEEPCurate for outcomes. Lead with the strongest, results-focused ones.
11Awards / recognition FOLDDon't dedicate a full section to nominations. Fold into the trust strip with honest framing.

Beyond the homepage

Three things the structure needs that aren't pages yet

SEO + Sales

Comparison pages

Elux vs freelancer · vs traditional agency · vs in-house. Pure SEO + sales-enablement. These rank and they close deals. (Eleken has these.)

SEO engine

Blog / resource hub up front

Your blog is buried. The 82-article SEO calendar already in motion is the funnel — surface it hard on homepage + footer.

Conversion

Qualification section

"We are not for everyone." Repels mismatched leads, signals confidence, and makes the right buyer feel chosen.

The homepage gets the wow. These pages get the traffic and do the convincing at scale. Eleken's 100+ articles + industry + comparison pages are why they rank — not their homepage.

The launch punch-list

Do it in this order

P0 · Ship-blockers
  • Replace the random YouTube videos with real work or static visuals
  • Fix all typos — Proccess, Enterprice, Bahance + full sweep
  • QA the scroll/motion on a real Android + throttled network
P1 · Before real traffic
  • Add the "How AI-native solves it" section — close the strategic gap
  • Put "AI-native" in the hero's first line
  • Vary the per-client stats with real numbers
  • Curate testimonials for outcomes
  • Tighten the stats-section dead space
P2 · The growth layer
  • Build industries as real indexable pages
  • Add comparison pages (vs freelancer / agency / in-house)
  • Surface the blog on homepage + fatten the footer
  • Add the "who we're NOT for" qualifier
  • Add a /how-we-work detail page

P0 = launch-safe today. P1 = the repositioning lands. P2 = the SEO + conversion engine that compounds.
